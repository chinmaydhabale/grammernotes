## 1. Pronoun ki Basic Definition aur Personal Pronoun Cases Table

### 🔍 Pronoun kya hota hai?
- **Definition**: Noun ke repetition ko rokne ke liye jo shabd Noun ki jagah par use hota hai, use **Pronoun (सर्वनाम)** kehte hain.
- **Fun Analogy (Role in Sentence)**: Agar Noun **"The Hero / Lead Actor"** hai, to Pronoun sentence ka **"The Stunt Double"** hai — jab Hero (Noun) baar-baar aakar thak jata hai, to Pronoun aakar uski jagah sambhalta hai taaki sentence sundar aur natural lage.
  - *Without Pronoun*: *Rahul called me. Rahul told me that Rahul was going to Rahul's office.* (Awkward & repetitive ❌)
  - *With Pronoun*: *Rahul called me. **He** told me that **he** was going to **his** office.* (Clean & Natural ✅)

---

### 📊 Master Personal Pronoun Cases Table:

| Person | Subjective / Nominative Case (Subject banta hai) | Objective / Accusative Case (Verb/Prep ka Object banta hai) | Possessive Adjective (+ Noun aayega) | Possessive Pronoun (Akela aayega, No Noun) | Reflexive / Emphatic Pronoun |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1st Person (Singular)** | **I** (मैं) | **Me** (मुझे/मुझको) | **My** (+ Noun) | **Mine** (मेरा) | **Myself** (खुद/स्वयं) |
| **1st Person (Plural)** | **We** (हम) | **Us** (हमें/हमको) | **Our** (+ Noun) | **Ours** (हमारा) | **Ourselves** |
| **2nd Person (Singular/Plural)** | **You** (आप/तुम) | **You** (आपको/तुम्हें) | **Your** (+ Noun) | **Yours** (आपका/तुम्हारा) | **Yourself** (Singular) / **Yourselves** (Plural) |
| **3rd Person (Male Singular)** | **He** (वह) | **Him** (उसे/उसको) | **His** (+ Noun) | **His** (उसका) | **Himself** |
| **3rd Person (Female Singular)** | **She** (वह) | **Her** (उसे/उसको) | **Her** (+ Noun) | **Hers** (उसका) | **Herself** |
| **3rd Person (Neuter/Non-Living)** | **It** (यह) | **It** (इसे) | **Its** (+ Noun) | — | **Itself** |
| **3rd Person (Plural All)** | **They** (वे/वो) | **Them** (उन्हें/उनको) | **Their** (+ Noun) | **Theirs** (उनका) | **Themselves** (NOT *theirselves* ❌) |

#### ⚠️ Possessive Adjective vs. Possessive Pronoun ka Difference:
- **Possessive Adjective** ke baad **Noun aana zaroori hai**:
  - *This is **my car**.* ✅ (*my* ke baad *car* aaya)
  - *That is **his book**.* ✅
- **Possessive Pronoun** ke baad **Noun KABHI NAHI aata** (yeh khud Noun ko replace karta hai):
  - *This car is **mine**.* ✅ (NOT *mine car* ❌)
  - *That house is **his**.* ✅
  - *These books are **hers / theirs / ours / yours**.* ✅

## 2. Reflexive aur Emphatic Pronouns

### 🔍 Reflexive vs Emphatic Pronoun:
1. **Reflexive Pronoun**: Jab Subject dwara kiya gaya kaam (action) ghoom kar wapas Subject par hi pade (Subject aur Object dono **same vyakti** hon).
   - *I taught **myself** how to cook.* (Maine khud ko sikhaya)
   - *I hurt **myself** while exercising.* (Maine khud ko chot pahunchayi)
2. **Emphatic Pronoun**: Jab pronoun sirf subject par **zor (emphasis) dene** ke liye use ho. Agar ise sentence se hata bhi dein, to sentence ka basic meaning nahi badalta.
   - *They decorated the room **themselves**.* (Unhone khud apne haathon se kamra sajaya)
   - *She **herself** told me the truth.*

---

### 🚫 Cardinal Rule 1: Reflexive Pronoun KABHI Akela Subject nahi ban sakta!
Introduction me ya sentence ke subject me *Myself* use karna grammatically 100% galat hai:
- *Myself Rahul.* ❌ → * **I am Rahul** / **My name is Rahul**.* ✅
- *Suhas and **myself** are going to give a presentation.* ❌  
  → *Suhas and **I** are going to give a presentation.* ✅
- * **Myself** and the principal will distribute the prizes.* ❌  
  → *The principal and **I** will distribute the prizes.* ✅
- *Kanvi and **myself** have completed the work.* ❌  
  → *Kanvi and **I** have completed the work.* ✅
- *The manager asked Rohan and **myself** to submit the report.* ❌  
  → *The manager asked Rohan and **me** to submit the report.* ✅ (Object position me *me* aayega!)

---

### 📌 Cardinal Rule 2: Verbs that MANDATORILY require Reflexive Pronoun
Agar in verbs ke baad koi **Direct Object nahi diya ho**, to inke baad **Reflexive Pronoun lagana anivarya (mandatory)** hai:

> **Avail (oneself of), Adapt, Adjust, Reconcile, Acquit, Amuse, Exert, Pride, Resign, Introduce, Enjoy.**

- *Examples*:
  - *You must **avail yourself of** this golden opportunity.* ✅ (NOT *You must avail this opportunity* ❌)
  - *He **adapted himself** to the new environment.* ✅
  - *She **prided herself on** her academic achievements.* ✅
  - *They **enjoyed themselves** at the party.* ✅ (Jab object na ho → *enjoyed themselves*)
  - *He **enjoyed the movie**.* ✅ (Yahan direct object *the movie* moojood hai, isliye reflexive nahi aayega!)

---

### 🚫 Cardinal Rule 3: Verbs that NEVER take Reflexive Pronouns
In verbs ke sath Reflexive Pronoun lagana strictly galat hai:

> **Keep, Break, Stop, Turn, Qualify, Bathe, Move, Rest, Hide, Shave.**

- *Error*: *He **hid himself** behind the curtain.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *He **hid** behind the curtain.* ✅
- *Error*: *She **kept herself** away from the controversy.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *She **kept** away from the controversy.* ✅
- *Error*: *He **qualified himself** in the examination.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *He **qualified** in the examination.* ✅

## 3. Reciprocal Pronouns: Each Other vs. One Another

Reciprocal Pronoun aapsi sambandh (mutual relationship/action) ko darshate hain:

```
                  ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │          RECIPROCAL PRONOUNS           │
                  └───────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                      │
                ┌─────────────────────┴─────────────────────┐
                ▼                                           ▼
      【 EACH OTHER 】                             【 ONE ANOTHER 】
      • Exactly TWO (2) persons/things             • MORE THAN TWO (3+) persons/things
```

1. **Each other (Sirf 2 vyaktiyon ya vastuo ke liye)**:
   - *The two rival companies have been competing with **each other**.* ✅ (NOT *one another* ❌)
   - *Both the players were so angry that they refused to talk to **each other**.* ✅
   - *The two siblings borrowed **each other's** books.* ✅
   - *Ravi and Priya love **each other**.* ✅

2. **One another (2 se zyada / 3+ vyaktiyon ya vastuo ke liye)**:
   - *It is essential for teammates (many) to support **one another**.* ✅ (NOT *each other* ❌)
   - *All the students in the class helped **one another** during exams.* ✅
   - *The four brothers always quarrel with **one another**.* ✅

## 4. Order of Pronouns: 231 vs 123 Rules & Combined Possessive Cases

Jab ek hi sentence me alag-alag persons ke pronouns (`1st: I/We`, `2nd: You`, `3rd: He/She/They`) ek sath Subject bankar aayein:

### 🌟 Case A: Normal / Positive / Neutral Context (Singular Pronouns) ⟹ **2 - 3 - 1 Rule**
Aam bolchal, achhe kaam, ya normal baaton me manners ka rule kehta hai: **Pehle saamne wale ko (2nd), phir teesre ko (3rd), aur aakhiri me khud ko (1st) rakho!**

$$\mathbf{2nd\ Person\ (You) → 3rd\ Person\ (He/She/It/Name) → 1st\ Person\ (I)}$$

- *Error*: *I, you and he will manage the project.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: * **You, he and I** will manage the project.* ✅
- *Error*: *He and you have been selected for the interview.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: * **You and he** have been selected for the interview.* ✅
- *Error*: *You and me have done a great job.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: * **You and I** have done a great job.* ✅
- *Error*: *I, you and she will visit the museum.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: * **You, she and I** will visit the museum.* ✅

---

### ⚡ Case B: Negative Context / Mistake / Guilt / Confession ⟹ **1 - 2 - 3 Rule**
Jab koi galti (Blunder), apradh (Crime), nuksan (Loss), ya paap (Sin) accept karna ho, to **sabse pehle khud aage aao (1st), phir saamne wale ko (2nd), phir teesre ko (3rd) rakho!**

$$\mathbf{1st\ Person\ (I) → 2nd\ Person\ (You) → 3rd\ Person\ (He/She/They)}$$

- *Error*: *You, he and I are responsible for the loss/damage.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: * **I, you and he** are responsible for the loss/damage.* ✅
- *Error*: *You and I committed the blunder.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: * **I and you** committed the blunder.* ✅
- *Error*: *He and I stole the document.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: * **I and he** stole the document.* ✅

---

### 👥 Case C: Plural Pronouns (We, You, They) ⟹ Hamesha **1 - 2 - 3 Rule**
Agar saare pronouns **Plural** hon (`We, You, They`), to context chahe positive ho ya negative, hamesha **1-2-3** order hi lagega:

$$\mathbf{We\ (1st) → You\ (2nd) → They\ (3rd)}$$

- * **We, you and they** should work together to achieve the sales target.* ✅ (NOT *We, they and you* ❌)

---

### 👑 Combined Pronouns ke sath Possessive Adjective Chunne ka Niyam (Priority Order):
Jab multiple persons jude hon, to aage unka common possessive kya lagega? Hamesha **Higher Priority Case** follow karo:

$$\mathbf{1st\ Person\ (Our) > 2nd\ Person\ (Your) > 3rd\ Person\ (Their)}$$

1. **Agar 1st Person (I / We) moojood hai**: Hamesha **"OUR"** lagega!
   - *You, he and I will commence **our** project.* ✅ (NOT *your* ❌, NOT *his* ❌)
   - *I, you and he will get **our** prize.* ✅
2. **Agar 1st Person nahi hai, lekin 2nd Person (You) moojood hai**: Hamesha **"YOUR"** lagega!
   - *You and he will complete **your** assignment.* ✅ (NOT *his* ❌, NOT *their* ❌)
   - *You and Rohit must submit **your** report.* ✅
3. **Agar sirf 3rd Person moojood hain**: Hamesha **"THEIR"** lagega!
   - *Rohit and Mohit have submitted **their** papers.* ✅

## 5. Rule of "LET" (Objective Case & Bare Infinitive)

"LET" ek Transitive Verb hai. Iske do sabse mahatvapurna exam rules hain:

### 📌 Rule 1: "LET" ke baad hamesha **Objective Case** (`me, him, her, us, them, you`) aata hai:
- *Error*: *Let **I** go.* ❌ → *Let **me** go.* ✅
- *Error*: *Let **he and she** finish the work.* ❌ → *Let **him and her** finish the work.* ✅
- *Error*: *Let **you and I** resolve this dispute.* ❌ → *Let **you and me** resolve this dispute.* ✅
- *Error*: *Let **she** take the lead.* ❌ → *Let **her** take the lead.* ✅

### 📌 Rule 2: "LET" ke baad hamesha **Bare Infinitive (Base Form - V1)** lagta hai (`to + V1` ya `s/es` KABHI NAHI lagta!):
- *Error*: *The manager let him **to explain** his story.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *The manager let him **explain** his story.* ✅
- *Error*: *She let her brother **to use** her laptop.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *She let her brother **use** her laptop.* ✅
- *Error*: *Let him **goes** ahead with the plan.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *Let him **go** ahead with the plan.* ✅
- *Error*: *Let me **to know** if you need assistance.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *Let me **know** if you need assistance.* ✅

## 6. Rule of Prepositions (Between, But/Except, For, With + Objective Case)

Kisi bhi **Preposition** ke baad aane wala Pronoun hamesha **Objective Case** me hota hai:

$$\mathbf{Preposition + Objective\ Case\ (me,\ him,\ her,\ us,\ them,\ you)}$$

- **Between**:
  - *Error*: *There is no secret between **you and I**.* ❌  
    → *Correction*: *There is no secret between **you and me**.* ✅
  - *Error*: *Between **you and he**, the decision was made.* ❌  
    → *Correction*: *Between **you and him**, the decision was made.* ✅
- **But / Except (के सिवाय / को छोड़कर)**:
  - Jab "But" preposition (except) ka kaam kare, to aage Objective Case aayega:
  - *Error*: *There is no one in the boardroom but **I** who understands the situation.* ❌  
    → *Correction*: *There is no one in the boardroom but **me** who understands...* ✅
  - *Everyone attended the meeting except **him and her**.* ✅
- **Other Prepositions (To, For, With, By)**:
  - *This has been understood by Kanvi and **me**.* ✅ (NOT *by Kanvi and I* ❌)
  - *The manager distributed the work between **him and you**.* ✅

## 7. Rule of "To Be" Verbs (It is / It was + Subjective Case)

Jab sentence me `It is / It was / It were` ke baad aane wale pronoun ka use ho (Complement of 'Be' Verb):

$$\mathbf{It\ is\ /\ It\ was + Subjective\ Case\ (I,\ he,\ she,\ they,\ we) + Relative\ Pronoun + Verb}$$

- **Rule**: 'Be' verb ke baad **Subjective Pronoun** aata hai, aur aage aane wali Relative Pronoun ke baad ki verb isi Subjective Pronoun ke person/number ke anusaar lagti hai!

### Examples & Corrections:
1. *It is **me**.* ❌ → *It is **I**.* ✅
2. *It was **him** who broke the window.* ❌  
   → *It was **he** who broke the window.* ✅
3. *It is **them** who are leading the protest.* ❌  
   → *It is **they** who are leading the protest.* ✅
4. *It is **me who is** responsible for the delay.* ❌  
   → *It is **I who am** responsible for the delay.* ✅ (I ke sath `am` lagta hai!)
5. *It is **we who has** been selected.* ❌  
   → *It is **we who have** been selected.* ✅ (We ke sath `have` lagta hai!)
6. *If I were **him**, I would accept the offer.* ❌  
   → *If I were **he**, I would accept the offer.* ✅
7. *The winner of the lottery was **he**.* ✅ (NOT *was him* ❌)

## 8. Comparison Rules with "THAN" and "AS...AS"

### 🅰️ Subject vs. Subject Comparison ⟹ Subjective Case:
Jab sentence me do Subjects ki tulna ho rahi ho:
- *He is taller than **I (am)**.* ✅ (Formal / Exam Rule: *than I*, NOT *than me* ❌)
- *No analyst in the department understands markets better than **she (does)**.* ✅ (NOT *than her* ❌)
- *No researcher is as meticulous as **she (is)**.* ✅ (NOT *as her* ❌)
- *Although he is younger, he earns twice as much as **I (do)**.* ✅ (NOT *as me* ❌)
- *Rohan is definitely more talented than **she (is)**.* ✅ (NOT *than her* ❌)

### 🅱️ Object vs. Object Comparison ⟹ Objective Case:
Jab sentence me do Objects ki tulna ho rahi ho:
- *I know you better than **him**.* (Meaning: Main aapko utna acche se jaanta hoon jitna us ladke ko nahi jaanta → You vs Him tulna).
- *I know you better than **he (knows you)**.* (Meaning: Wo ladka aapko jitna jaanta hai, main usse zyada jaanta hoon → I vs He tulna).
- *My brother loves our dog more than **me**.* (Brother dog se zyada pyaar karta hai mujhse kam).
- *My brother loves our dog more than **I (do)**.* (Brother mujhse zyada dog ko pyaar karta hai).

## 9. The Golden Rule of "ONE"

Jab sentence ka Subject Indefinite Pronoun **"ONE"** ho (jiska matlab hota hai "Kisi bhi vyakti ko / har kisi ko"):

$$\mathbf{One\ (Subject) ⟹ One\ (Object) ⟹ One's\ (Possessive) ⟹ Oneself\ (Reflexive)}$$

- **Golden Rule**: Pura sentence shuru se aakhir tak **ONE** ki family me hi chalega. Isme beech me *he, him, his, himself, they, their, themselves* lagana strictly prohibited hai!

### Examples & Exam Corrections:
1. *One should always respect **his** elders and seek **their** blessings.* ❌  
   → *One should always respect **one's** elders and seek **their** blessings.* ✅
2. *One cannot expect **themselves** to succeed without effort.* ❌  
   → *One cannot expect **oneself** to succeed without effort.* ✅
3. *One must focus on **his** goals and work hard.* ❌  
   → *One must focus on **one's** goals and work hard.* ✅
4. *One should always believe in **his** capabilities.* ❌  
   → *One should always believe in **one's** capabilities.* ✅
5. *One should be careful about what **he** says in a formal gathering.* ❌  
   → *One should be careful about what **one** says in a formal gathering.* ✅
6. *One should always be mindful of **their** surroundings.* ❌  
   → *One should always be mindful of **one's** surroundings.* ✅

## 10. "It" vs. "Its" vs. "It's" vs. "Its'"

| Form | Grammar Category | Meaning & Function | Correct Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **It** | Pronoun (Subject/Object) | Non-living, janwar ya chhote bachhe ke liye | *I bought a phone; **it** works great.* |
| **Its** | **Possessive Adjective** | Uska / Iska (Adhikar darshata hai, No apostrophe!) | • *The company revised **its** policies.*<br>• *The cat licked **its** paw.*<br>• *The firm lost **its** credibility.* |
| **It's** | **Contraction (Short Form)** | **It is** ya **It has** | • * **It's** a beautiful day.* (= It is)<br>• * **It's** been observed.* (= It has)<br>• * **It's** evident that...* (= It is) |
| **Its'** | ❌ **Invalid Word** | English me aisa koi word exist hi nahi karta! | 100% Grammatically Wrong! |

### Fill in the Blanks / Spot the Error:
- *The firm lost **its** credibility because of **its** failing to adapt.* ✅
- * **It's** a known fact that the firm has lost **its** competitive edge.* ✅
- *The machine stopped functioning because **its** (NOT *it's* ❌) battery was drained.*
- *The institution changed **its** policies because **its** (NOT *it's* ❌) reputation was declining.*

## 11. Indefinite Pronouns

### 1. Singular Indefinite Pronouns (Always Singular Verb & Singular Pronoun `his/her`):
> **Everyone, Everybody, Someone, Somebody, Anyone, Anybody, No one, Nobody, Everything, Something, Anything, Nothing, Each, Either, Neither, One.**

- *Everyone in the auditorium **was** applauding.* (NOT *were* ❌)
- *Somebody has left **his** (NOT *their* ❌) umbrella in the office.*
- *Nobody **knows** (NOT *know* ❌) the reason behind the cancellation.*
- *Neither of the candidates **has** prepared **himself**.* (NOT *have*, NOT *themselves* ❌)
- *Each of the researchers **has contributed**.* (NOT *have* ❌)

---

### 2. Plural Indefinite Pronouns (Always Plural Verb & Plural Pronoun `their`):
> **Many, Few, Both, Several, Others.**

- *Many **have** applied for the job.*
- *Few **know** the full story.*
- *Both **are** invited.*
- *Several **were** absent today.*

---

### 3. Variable Indefinite Pronouns (SANAM Rule):
> **S**ome, **A**ll, **N**one, **A**ny, **M**ost

Inka number unke baad aane wale **Noun** par depend karta hai:

$$\mathbf{SANAM + Uncountable\ Noun ⟹ Singular\ Verb}$$
$$\mathbf{SANAM + Plural\ Countable\ Noun ⟹ Plural\ Verb}$$

- **All**:
  - *All of the water **is** gone.* (Uncountable → Singular)
  - *All of the people **are** waiting.* (Countable → Plural)
- **Some**:
  - *Some of the evidence **is** lost.* (Uncountable → Singular)
  - *Some of the books **are** missing.* (Countable → Plural)
- **Most**:
  - *Most of the information **is** accurate.* (Uncountable → Singular)
  - *Most of the liquidity in the market **has** dried up.* (Liquidity = Uncountable → *has*, NOT *have* ❌)
  - *Most of the members **are** here.* (Countable → Plural)
- **None**:
  - *None of the milk **was** spilled.* (Uncountable → Singular)
  - *None of the players **were** tired.* (Countable → Plural)

## 12. "Each" vs. "Every" Deep Comparison Table & Multiple "Each" Rule

### 📊 Master Comparison Table:

| Feature | Each | Every |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Focus** | **Individualistic**: Har ek member ko alag-alag ("one by one") dekhta hai | **Collective**: Pure group ko ek single unit ki tarah dekhta hai |
| **Minimum Group Size** | **Two or more (2+)**: Do vyaktiyon ke liye use ho sakta hai | **Three or more (3+)**: Do ke liye KABHI use nahi hota! |
| **Parts of Speech** | **Pronoun aur Adjective** dono ka kaam karta hai | **Sirf Adjective** ka kaam karta hai (Pronoun nahi hota) |
| **"Of" Construction** | **Allowed**: *"Each of the students..."* ✅ | **Strictly Forbidden**: *"Every of the students..."* ❌ |
| **Number Context** | **Definite**: Jab total count limited/known ho | **Indefinite**: Jab sankhya bahut badi ya unknown ho |
| **Intervals / Frequency** | ❌ Cannot be used | **Mandatory**: *"every 10 minutes", "every 5 km"* ✅ |
| **With Adverbs** | ❌ Cannot pair | **Compatible**: *"almost every", "nearly every"* ✅ |
| **With Abstract Nouns** | ❌ Cannot pair | **Commonly used**: *"every reason", "every effort"* ✅ |

#### ⚠️ Error Spotting based on Each vs. Every:
1. * **Every of the** five participants was awarded.* ❌ → * **Each of the** five participants...* ✅
2. * **Almost each** student has completed the task.* ❌ → * **Almost every** student...* ✅
3. *There are tall buildings on **every side** of the street.* ❌ → *...on **each side** (gali ke 2 hi sides hote hain!).* ✅
4. *A bus leaves **each ten minutes**.* ❌ → *...leaves **every ten minutes**.* ✅
5. *I have **each reason** to believe him.* ❌ → *I have **every reason** to believe him.* ✅

---

### 👑 Multiple "Each / Every" Rule:
Jab do nouns `and` se jude hon aur dono ke aage `Each` ya `Every` laga ho:

$$\mathbf{Each\ /\ Every + Noun\ 1 + and + Each\ /\ Every + Noun\ 2 ⟹ Singular\ Verb + Singular\ Pronoun\ (his/her)}$$

- *Error*: *Each boy and each teacher have submitted **their** report.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *Each boy and each teacher **has** submitted **his** report.* ✅
- *Error*: *Each man and each soldier carried **their** own bag.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *Each man and each soldier carried **his** own bag.* ✅
- *Error*: *Each officer and each clerk **were** praised for **their** work.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *Each officer and each clerk **was** praised for **his** work.* ✅
- *Error*: *Every man and every woman came out of **their houses**.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *Every man and every woman came out of **his/her house**.* ✅

## 13. Relative Pronouns: Who vs. Whom vs. Whose vs. Which vs. That

### 🔍 1. "Who" vs. "Whom" Master Technique (Subject vs. Object):
- **Who**: Subject pronoun hota hai (He / She / They ka substitute).
- **Whom**: Object pronoun hota hai (Him / Her / Them ka substitute).

#### 💡 The Subject-Verb Counting Trick:
Sentence me saare Subjects ($S$) aur saari Finite Verbs ($V$) count karo:
1. **Agar Verbs ki sankhya Subjects se zyada ho ($V > S$)**: Ek Subject kam pad raha hai ⟹ **WHO** lagega!
2. **Agar Verbs aur Subjects barabar hon ($V = S$)**: Subject pura hai, sirf Object chahiye ⟹ **WHOM** lagega!

- *Example 1*: *He is the person **who/whom** developed this project.*
  - $V1$ = *is*, $S1$ = *He*
  - $V2$ = *developed*, $S2$ = missing!
  - $V > S ⟹$ **WHO** aayega! ✅
- *Example 2*: *He is the person **who/whom** I helped.*
  - $V1$ = *is*, $S1$ = *He*
  - $V2$ = *helped*, $S2$ = *I*
  - $V = S ⟹$ **WHOM** aayega! ✅
- *Example 3 (Tough Exam Question)*: *The manager **whom** I thought would lead the team has resigned.* ❌
  - $V1$ = *has resigned* ($S1$ = *The manager*)
  - $V2$ = *thought* ($S2$ = *I*)
  - $V3$ = *would lead* ($S3$ = missing!)
  - $V (3) > S (2) ⟹$ **WHO** aayega!
  - → *The manager **who** I thought would lead the team has resigned.* ✅

---

### 🔍 2. "Whose" vs. "Of Which":
- **Whose**: Living beings ke possessive ke liye use hota hai (*The candidate whose resume is on the table*).
- **Of which**: Non-living objects ke liye traditional grammar me use hota hai:
  - *The antique clock, **the pendulum of which** is made of gold, is priceless.* ✅
  - *The skyscraper, **the height of which** is 500 meters.* ✅ (Modern English me *whose height* bhi accept hone laga hai).

---

## 14. Special Rules of "THAT" (Superlative & Specific Trigger Words)

In 4 conditions me Relative Pronoun ke roop me `Who` ya `Which` ka use **strictly prohibited** hota hai — sirf aur sirf **"THAT"** ka use hota hai:

### 1. Superlative Degree of Adjective ke baad:
- *He is the **best** manager **who** I have worked with.* ❌ → *...manager **that** I have worked with.* ✅
- *This is the **highest** mountain peak **which** we scaled.* ❌ → *...peak **that** we scaled.* ✅
- *Mr. Sharma is the **ablest** clerk **who** we have.* ❌ → *...clerk **that** we have.* ✅
- *This is the **most expensive** car **which** I have seen.* ❌ → *...car **that** I have seen.* ✅

---

### 2. Specific Trigger Words ke baad:
> **All, Any, Only, The same, None, Nothing, Little, Few, Anything, Everything, Much, Nobody, Such.**

- *All **which** the manager said was recorded.* ❌ → *All **that** the manager said...* ✅
- *All **that** glitters is not gold.* ✅ (NOT *All which* ❌)
- *There is nothing **which** can stop him.* ❌ → *There is nothing **that** can stop him.* ✅
- *This is the same book **which** I bought yesterday.* ❌ → *This is the same book **that** I bought...* ✅
- *He is the only one **who** has arrived.* ❌ → *He is the only one **that** has arrived.* ✅

---

### 3. Interrogative Pronoun (`Who / What`) se sentence start hone par (Double Who/Which avoid karne ke liye):
- * **Who** is the man **who** does not desire happiness?* ❌  
  → * **Who** is the man **that** does not desire happiness?* ✅
- * **What** is it **which** troubles you?* ❌ → * **What** is it **that** troubles you?* ✅

---

### 4. Jab ek Living Being (Man) aur ek Non-living/Animal dono `and` se jude hon:
- *The lady and her pet dog **that** entered the sanctuary.* ✅ (Kyunki Lady ke liye *who* aur Dog ke liye *which* alag-alag nahi laga sakte, isliye common **that** lagta hai).

---

## 15. Interrogative Pronouns: "What" vs. "Which"

| Question Word | Condition / Scope | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **What** | **Unlimited / Infinite / Open Choices** (Jab options open ya bahut saare hon) | • * **What** do you want to eat?* (Menu me hazaron cheezein ho sakti hain)<br>• * **What** is your favourite colour?*<br>• * **What** time is it?*<br>• * **What** is your phone number?* |
| **Which** | **Limited / Restricted Choices** (Jab options pehle se limited ya samne rakhe hon) | • * **Which** dish do you want to eat — pizza or burger?*<br>• * **Which** dress should I wear — the red or the blue?*<br>• * **Which** eye was hurt?* (Aankhein 2 hi hoti hain)<br>• * **Which** is the correct option in this MCQ?* (Exam me options limited hote hain!) |

## 16. Latin Adjectives & Comparative Structures

Aise Adjectives jo Latin origin ke hain aur `-ior` ya specific endings par khatam hote hain:

> **Senior, Junior, Superior, Inferior, Prior, Posterior, Anterior, Ulterior, Preferable, Elder.**

### 🚫 2 Mandatory Rules for Latin Adjectives:
1. Inke sath comparison me **"TO"** lagta hai, `than` **KABHI NAHI** lagta!
2. Inke aage `more` ya `most` lagana **Superfluous (Galat)** hai!

- *Error*: *He is **senior than** all his colleagues.* ❌ → *He is **senior to** all his colleagues.* ✅
- *Error*: *This solution is **more preferable than** that one.* ❌ → *This solution is **preferable to** that one.* ✅
- *Error*: *Honesty is **superior than** dishonesty.* ❌ → *Honesty is **superior to** dishonesty.* ✅
- *Error*: *She is **junior than** me in rank.* ❌ → *She is **junior to** me in rank.* ✅
- *Error*: *They reached the station **prior than** others.* ❌ → *...reached the station **prior to** others.* ✅

---

## 17. Demonstrative Pronouns: "That of" and "Those of" (Logical Comparison)

Tulna (Comparison) hamesha do **saman (equal/logical) cheezon** ke beech honi chahiye. Noun ke repetition ko bachane ke liye:
- **Singular Noun replacement** ⟹ **"THAT OF"**
- **Plural Noun replacement** ⟹ **"THOSE OF"**

### Examples & Exam Corrections:
- *Error*: *The climate of Mumbai is hotter than **Delhi**.* ❌ (Mumbai ke climate ki tulna Delhi shahar se nahi ho sakti!)  
  → *Correction*: *The climate of Mumbai is hotter than **that of Delhi**.* ✅
- *Error*: *The roads of Delhi are wider than **Mumbai**.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *The roads of Delhi are wider than **those of Mumbai**.* ✅
- *Error*: *The vision of the new startup is far more unique than **its competitors**.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *The vision of the new startup is far more unique than **that of its competitors**.* ✅

## 19. Quick Revision Summary (Exam Cheat Sheet)

| Concept / Keyword | Quick Rule Formula |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Personal Pronoun Cases** | Subject position → Subjective (`I, he, she, they`) \| Object position → Objective (`me, him, her, them`) |
| **Reflexive as Subject** | *Myself* akela Subject KABHI nahi ban sakta → Use *I* / *My name is* |
| **Reflexive Verbs** | *Avail of, Adapt, Adjust, Pride, Resign, Enjoy* (without object) → Mandatory Reflexive |
| **Non-Reflexive Verbs** | *Keep, Break, Stop, Turn, Qualify, Hide, Bathe* → NO Reflexive |
| **Reciprocal Pronouns** | `Each other` = Exactly 2 \| `One another` = 3 or more |
| **Order of Persons (Singular)** | Positive Context → **2 - 3 - 1** (`You, he and I`) \| Negative/Fault → **1 - 2 - 3** (`I, you and he`) |
| **Order of Persons (Plural)** | Hamesha **1 - 2 - 3** (`We, you and they`) |
| **Combined Possessive** | Priority: **Our** (1st present) > **Your** (2nd present) > **Their** (Only 3rd) |
| **After "LET"** | Always **Objective Case** (`me, him, her, us, them`) + **Bare Infinitive (V1)** |
| **After Prepositions** | *Between, But, Except, With, By, For* → Always **Objective Case** (`you and me`) |
| **After "To Be"** | *It is / It was* → Always **Subjective Case** (`It is I who am`, `It was he who`) |
| **Than / As...As Comparison** | Subject vs Subject → **Subjective Case** (`taller than I`, `better than she`) |
| **Rule of "ONE"** | `One` → `One's` (Possessive) → `Oneself` (Reflexive) (No *his/himself/their*) |
| **Its vs It's** | `Its` = Possessive Adjective (Uska) \| `It's` = It is / It has \| `Its'` = 100% INVALID |
| **Indefinite Pronoun Concord** | *Everyone, Somebody, Each, Neither* → **Singular Verb + his** |
| **SANAM Rule** | *Some, All, None, Any, Most* → Uncountable (Singular) \| Plural Countable (Plural) |
| **Each vs Every** | `Each of the` ✅ \| `Every of the` ❌ \| Intervals/Adverbs → Use `Every` |
| **Multiple Each / Every** | *Each boy and each girl* → **Singular Verb + his** |
| **Who vs Whom** | Count $V$ & $S$: If $V > S ⟹$ **Who** (Subject) \| If $V = S ⟹$ **Whom** (Object) |
| **Mandatory "THAT"** | Superlatives (`best, highest`), Triggers (`all, same, only, none, nothing`), Interrogatives (`Who...that`) |
| **What vs Which** | `What` = Unlimited Choices \| `Which` = Limited / Restricted Choices (MCQs, 2 options) |
| **Latin Adjectives** | *Senior, Junior, Superior, Inferior, Preferable* → Takes **TO** (Never *than*, Never *more*) |
| **That of / Those of** | Avoid illogical comparison → Singular: *that of* \| Plural: *those of* |
