## 1. Introduction to Moods in English Grammar

### 🔍 Mood kya hota hai?
- **Definition**: Mood sentence me speaker ke **attitude (मनोभाव/इरादे)** ko darshata hai — ki sentence ek simple fact hai, command hai, question hai, shart (condition) hai, ya koi aisi iccha/sujhav hai jo abhi reality nahi hai.
- **Kya har sentence me normal Tense lagta hai?**: NAHI! Subjunctive Mood me normal Tense ke rules apply nahi hote, balki **Pure Base Verb Form** lagti hai.

---

### 📊 5 Main Types of Mood in English:

| No. | Mood Type | Function / Purpose | Example Sentence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Indicative Mood** | **Facts, Reality, ya Opinions** batana (Normal sentences) | • *The company reported record profits.* (Fact)<br>• *She believes that AI will transform education.* |
| **2** | **Imperative Mood** | **Aadesh (Command), Prarthna (Request), ya Nirdesh (Instruction)** | • *Submit the assignment before Monday.* (Instruction)<br>• *Please close the window quietly.* (Request) |
| **3** | **Interrogative Mood**| **Prashna (Questions)** poochna | • *Why did the market crash suddenly?*<br>• *Have you completed the report?* |
| **4** | **Conditional Mood** | **Shart par aadharit sambhavna (Condition-based results)** | • *If interest rates rise, businesses may reduce investment.*<br>• *I would travel abroad if I had money.* |
| **5** | **Subjunctive Mood**  | **Sujhav (Suggestions), Mang (Demands), Anivaryata (Necessity), ya Kalpana** | • *The doctor suggested that he **rest** for a week.*<br>• *If I **were** the manager, I would cut costs.* |

## 2. Conditional Sentences: 4 Master Types Matrix

Conditional Sentences me **DO (2) Clauses** hote hain:
1. **If-Clause (Condition / शर्त)**
2. **Main Clause (Result / परिणाम)**

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                CONDITIONAL SENTENCES MASTER MATRIX                                     │
├─────────────┬───────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Type        │ Condition Nature  │ If-Clause (Tense Structure)     │ Main Clause (Result Structure)      │
├─────────────┼───────────────────┼────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤
│ **Zero**    │ **Universal Fact**| **Simple Present (`V1/V5`)**   | **Simple Present (`V1/V5`)**        │
│ **First**   │ **Likely Future** | **Simple Present (`V1/V5`)**   | **`will / can / may + V1 (Base)`**  │
│ **Second**  │ **Unreal Present**| **Simple Past (`V2 / WERE`)**  | **`would / could / might + V1`**    │
│ **Third**   │ **Past Regret**   | **Past Perfect (`HAD + V3`)**  | **`would / could have + V3`**       │
└─────────────┴───────────────────┴────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 3. Zero Conditional (Scientific Facts & Universal Truths)

Jab condition aur result dono **100% pakke scientific facts, universal laws, ya general truths** hon:

$$\mathbf{If + Simple\ Present\ (V1/V5)\ →\ Simple\ Present\ (V1/V5)}$$

- *Rule*: Result clause me `will` lagana **GALAT** hota hai kyunki result koi future prediction nahi balki nischit scientific fact hai!
  - *Error*: *If the water reaches 100 degrees, it **will start** to boil.* ❌  
    → *Correction*: *If water reaches 100 degrees, it **starts** / **boils**.* ✅
  - *Error*: *If you mix yellow and blue, you **will get** green.* ❌  
    → *Correction*: *If you mix yellow and blue, you **get** green.* ✅
  - *If a person **exercises** regularly, he **stays** fit and healthy.* ✅
  - *If plants don't receive sunlight, they **die**.* ✅

---

## 4. First Conditional (Real / Likely Future Possibilities)

Jab future me kisi kaam ke hone ki **vastavik sambhavna (real possibility)** ho:

$$\mathbf{If + Simple\ Present\ (V1/V5)\ →\ Subject + WILL\ /\ CAN\ /\ MAY + V1}$$

### 🚫 The "Double WILL" Cardinal Trap:
If-clause ke andar `will` lagana **Strictly Prohibited** hai:
- *Error*: *If you **will help** me with these files, I will finish the report.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *If you **help** me with these files, I will finish the report.* ✅
- *Error*: *Provided that he **will pay** the fee, he will get the admit card.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *Provided that he **pays** the fee, he **will get** the admit card.* ✅
- *If she arrives early, we **will start** the session.* ✅

---

## 5. Second Conditional (Unreal / Imaginary Present or Future)

Jab koi sthiti **kalpanik (imaginary), asambhav, ya vartaman me unreal** ho:

$$\mathbf{If + Simple\ Past\ (V2\ /\ WERE)\ →\ Subject + WOULD\ /\ COULD + V1}$$

- **Rule**: If-clause me singular subject (`I, He, She, It`) ke sath bhi **hamesha "WERE"** lagta hai (*was* nahi!):
  - *Error*: *If I **was you**, I would not accept the offer.* ❌  
    → *Correction*: *If I **were you**, I would not accept the offer.* ✅
  - *Error*: *If she was more disciplined, she will excel.* ❌  
    → *Correction*: *If she **were** more disciplined, she **would excel**.* ✅
  - *If I **were rich**, I **would travel** the world.* ✅
  - *If they **trusted** the process, they **would see** better results.* ✅

---

## 6. Third Conditional (Unfulfilled Past Regret & Imagination)

Jab past ka koi aisa mauka jo chhut chuka ho aur ab hum sirf **pachtawe (regret) ya past imagination** ki baat kar rahe hon:

$$\mathbf{If + HAD + V3\ →\ Subject + WOULD\ HAVE\ /\ COULD\ HAVE + V3}$$

### 🚫 The "Double WOULD HAVE" Error (#1 Exam Trap!):
If-clause me `would have` lagana **100% Strictly Prohibited** hai:
- *Error*: *If he **would have worked** harder, he would have secured a better rank.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *If he **had worked** harder, he **would have secured** a better rank.* ✅
- *Error*: *Had the rescuers arrived on time, they **would save** the lives.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *Had the rescuers arrived on time, they **would have saved** the lives.* ✅
- *Error*: *If the engine had not failed, the plane **would land** safely.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *...the plane **would have landed** safely.* ✅
- *If the government **had intervened**, the crisis **would have eased**.* ✅

## 7. The "Unless" Rule

$$\mathbf{UNLESS = IF\ +\ NOT\ (जब\ तक\ कि\ नहीं)}$$

- **Rule**: Chunki `Unless` apne aap me pehle se Negative hota hai, isliye **Unless wale clause ke andar "NOT / DOES NOT / DID NOT" lagana Superfluous (100% Galat)** hai!

### Examples & Exam Corrections:
- *Error*: * **Unless he does not work hard**, he will not clear the exam.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: * **Unless he works hard**, he will not clear the exam.* ✅
- *Error*: *Unless the management **does not address** the issues, strikes will continue.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *Unless the management **addresses** the issues, strikes will continue.* ✅

---

## 8. The "Suppose If" Superfluous Error

`Suppose` aur `If` dono ka prayog shart ya kalpana darshane ke liye hota hai. In dono ko **ek sath sentence me use karna 100% Superfluous Error** hai:

$$\mathbf{Suppose\ if ⟹ 100\%\ WRONG\ ERROR!}$$
$$\mathbf{Use\ EITHER\ "Suppose"\ OR\ "If"\ (Never\ Both!)}$$

- *Error*: * **Suppose if** you won a lottery, what would you do with the money?* ❌  
  → *Correction*: * **Suppose** you won a lottery, what would you do?* ✅  
  → *Or*: * **If** you won a lottery, what would you do?* ✅

## 9. Conditional Inversion Rules (Removing "IF")

Advanced English aur Bank/SSC exams me `IF` ko hatakar Inversion structure banaya jata hai:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                     CONDITIONAL INVERSION PATTERNS                     │
├──────────────┬───────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────┤
│ Type         │ Normal "IF" Clause            │ Inverted Structure      │
├──────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
│ **Type 1**   │ *If it rains...*              │ **Should + Sub + V1**   │
│ (Should)     │                               │ * **Should it rain**, we shall stay...* |
├──────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
│ **Type 2**   │ *If I were the PM...*         │ **Were + Sub + Obj**    │
│ (Were)       │                               │ * **Were I the PM**, I would work...* |
├──────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤
│ **Type 3**   │ *If I had realized...*        │ **Had + Sub + V3**      │
│ (Had)        │                               │ * **Had I realized**, I would not have...* |
└──────────────┴───────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┘
```

### Examples & Exam Corrections:
1. *Should it rains, we shall stay indoors.* ❌  
   → * **Should it rain** (Base V1), we shall stay indoors.* ✅
2. *Were I the Prime Minister, I will work for the poor.* ❌  
   → * **Were I the Prime Minister, I would work** for the poor.* ✅ (Type 2 takes *would*!).
3. *Had I known the truth, I wouldn't have gone.* ✅ (Correct Type 3 Inversion).

## 10. Subjunctive Mood: Deep Concept & Philosophy

### 🔍 Present Subjunctive kya hai?
- Jab hum kisi baat ko **Fact ke roop me nahi**, balki ek **Wish (इच्छा), Suggestion (सुझाव), Demand (मांग), Mandate (आदेश), ya Anivaryata (Necessity)** ke roop me express karte hain, to wahan **SUBJUNCTIVE MOOD** lagta hai.
- **The Core Secret**: Subjunctive Mood me Verb par **Tense aur Subject (Singular/Plural) ka koi asar nahi padta**! Yeh hamesha **PURE BASE FORM (V1)** me rehti hai.

---

## 11. Present Subjunctive: Mandative Verbs & Trigger Adjectives

### 🅰️ Mandative Verbs List (Verbs of Demand / Suggestion / Advice):
> **Advise, Ask, Command, Demand, Insist, Order, Propose, Recommend, Request, Require, Suggest, Urge + THAT**

---

### 🅱️ Trigger Adjectives List (Adjectives of Necessity / Importance):
> **Essential, Important, Necessary, Vital, Crucial, Imperative, Mandatory, Urgent + THAT**

---

## 12. The Subjunctive Base Verb Rules

Jab bhi upar diye gaye Verbs ya Adjectives ke baad `THAT` clause aaye:

$$\mathbf{Trigger\ Word\ +\ THAT\ +\ Subject\ +\ PURE\ BASE\ VERB}$$

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                     SUBJUNCTIVE BASE VERB MATRIX                       │
├───────────────┬───────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────┤
│ Verb Nature   │ Normal Indicative (Wrong ❌)   │ Subjunctive (Correct ✅)│
├───────────────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┤
│ **Action**    │ *maintains, receives, pays*   │ **maintain, receive, pay** (Base V1) |
│ **State**     │ *is, are, was, were*          │ **BE** (Pure Base Verb) |
│ **Possession**│ *has, had*                    │ **HAVE** (Pure Base Verb)|
└───────────────┴───────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────┘
```

---

### 🔍 Category-wise Examples & Exam Corrections:

#### 1. Action Verbs ⟹ Pure Base Form V1 (No `-s/-es`!):
- *Error*: *The regulatory panel recommended that every financial institution **maintains** higher reserves.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *...recommended that every financial institution **maintain** higher reserves.* ✅
- *Error*: *The investigative panel urged that the whistleblower **receives** protection.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *...urged that the whistleblower **receive** protection.* ✅
- *Error*: *The law requires that every citizen **pays** taxes honestly.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *The law requires that every citizen **pay** taxes honestly.* ✅
- *Error*: *The doctor advised that he **rests**.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *The doctor advised that he **rest**.* ✅

---

#### 2. State of Being ⟹ Pure Base Verb "BE" (No *is / are / was / were*!):
- *Error*: *It is imperative that confidential audit findings **are disclosed** only to authorized officials.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *It is imperative that confidential audit findings **be disclosed** only to authorized officials.* ✅
- *Error*: *The audit division insisted that every discrepancy in expenditure records **is investigated**.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *...insisted that every discrepancy **be investigated** before the meeting.* ✅
- *Error*: *The judge ordered that the accused **is released**.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *The judge ordered that the accused **be released**.* ✅
- *Error*: *It is important that he **is present** at the meeting.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *It is important that he **be present** at the meeting.* ✅
- *Error*: *The doctor suggested that she **is admitted** immediately.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *The doctor suggested that she **be admitted** immediately.* ✅

---

#### 3. State of Possession ⟹ Pure Base Verb "HAVE" (No *has / had*!):
- *Error*: *It is necessary that every applicant for the strategic role **has** prior experience.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *It is necessary that every applicant **have** prior experience.* ✅
- *Error*: *It is essential that every student **has** a valid ID card.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *It is essential that every student **have** a valid ID card.* ✅
- *Error*: *The teacher recommended that he **has** more practice sessions.* ❌  
  → *Correction*: *The teacher recommended that he **have** more practice sessions.* ✅

---

## 13. Past Subjunctive: Unreal & Hypothetical "WERE"

Jab baat kisi aisi kalpana ki ho jo reality ke viprit ho (`If, Wish, As though, As if` ke sath):
- **Rule**: Har subject (I, He, She, It, We, They) ke sath **sirf aur sirf "WERE"** lagta hai (*was* nahi):
  - *If I **were** the manager, I would reduce unnecessary expenses.* ✅
  - *I wish she **were** here with us today.* ✅
  - *He speaks as though he **were** the owner of the company.* ✅

## 14. Master Practice Question Bank (50+ Exam-Level Questions with Explanations)

Original class notes aur practice sets se compiled sabhi questions ka step-by-step analysis:

---

### 📝 Set 1: Conditional Sentences Master Traps

1. **Question**: *Unless he (A) / does not work hard, (B) / he will not be able (C) / to clear the Mains exam. (D)*
   - **Error**: Part (B)
   - **Explanation**: Unless already negative hai → **"Unless he works hard"**.

2. **Question**: *If the sun rises, (A) / the fog disappeared (B) / and the birds start singing. (C)*
   - **Error**: Part (B)
   - **Explanation**: Zero conditional / Universal routine fact me Simple Present aayega → **"the fog disappears"**.

3. **Question**: *Would you have helped him (A) / if he had asked you (B) / for a loan yesterday? (C)*
   - **Error**: (E) No Error
   - **Explanation**: Type 3 conditional (If had + V3 → Would have + V3) bilkul accurate hai.

4. **Question**: *If she knows your mobile number, (A) / she would have called you (B) / long ago. (C)*
   - **Error**: Part (A)
   - **Explanation**: Result clause me 'would have called' hai, isliye If-clause me Past Perfect aayega → **"If she had known"**.

5. **Question**: *Were I the Prime Minister, (A) / I will certainly work (B) / for the welfare of the poor. (C)*
   - **Error**: Part (B)
   - **Explanation**: 'Were' (Type 2 conditional) ke sath 'would' lagta hai → **"I would certainly work"**.

6. **Question**: *If the water reaches (A) / one hundred degrees, (B) / it will start to boil immediately. (C)*
   - **Error**: Part (C)
   - **Explanation**: Scientific fact → Zero conditional → **"it starts to boil"**.

7. **Question**: *If I was you, (A) / I would not accept the offer (B) / without reading the contract. (C)*
   - **Error**: Part (A)
   - **Explanation**: Hypothetical conditional → **"If I were you"**.

8. **Question**: *Had the rescuers arrived on time, (A) / they would save the lives (B) / of the trekkers. (C)*
   - **Error**: Part (B)
   - **Explanation**: Type 3 inversion (Had + V3) → **"they would have saved"**.

9. **Question**: *If you will help me with these files, (A) / I will finish the report (B) / by the evening. (C)*
   - **Error**: Part (A)
   - **Explanation**: If-clause me 'will' nahi lagta → **"If you help me"**.

10. **Question**: *If you mix yellow and blue, (A) / you will always get green. (B)*
    - **Error**: Part (B)
    - **Explanation**: Zero conditional fact → **"you always get green"**.

11. **Question**: *Suppose if you won a lottery, (A) / what would you do (B) / with the money? (C)*
    - **Error**: Part (A)
    - **Explanation**: 'Suppose if' superfluous hai → **"Suppose you won"** ya **"If you won"**.

12. **Question**: *Should it rains, (A) / we shall stay indoors (B) / and study for the test. (C)*
    - **Error**: Part (A)
    - **Explanation**: Inversion 'Should' takes base V1 → **"Should it rain"**.

## 15. Quick Revision Summary (Exam Cheat Sheet)

| Concept / Keyword | Quick Rule Formula |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Zero Conditional** | `If + V1/V5` → `V1/V5` (Universal fact - Never use *will*) |
| **First Conditional** | `If + V1/V5` → `will + V1` (Never use *will* in If-clause!) |
| **Second Conditional** | `If + V2 / WERE` → `would + V1` (Unreal present - Always *were*) |
| **Third Conditional** | `If + HAD + V3` → `WOULD HAVE + V3` (Never *If + would have* ❌) |
| **Unless Rule** | `Unless = If not` → Never put *not / does not* inside unless clause |
| **Suppose if Trap** | Use **ONLY Suppose** OR **ONLY If** (Never together) |
| **Should Inversion** | `Should + Subject + Base V1` (*Should it rain*, NOT *rains*) |
| **Were Inversion** | `Were + Subject + Noun/Adj ..., Subject + WOULD + V1` |
| **Had Inversion** | `Had + Subject + V3 ..., Subject + WOULD HAVE + V3` |
| **Mandative Subjunctive Verbs** | *Recommend, Demand, Insist, Urge, Propose, Advise, Require + THAT* |
| **Subjunctive Adjectives** | *Essential, Important, Necessary, Vital, Imperative, Mandatory + THAT* |
| **Subjunctive Action Verbs** | Pure **Base Form V1** (*maintain, receive, pay* - Never *-s/-es*) |
| **Subjunctive State Verb** | Pure Base **BE** (*be disclosed, be investigated, be released*) |
| **Subjunctive Possession** | Pure Base **HAVE** (*have experience, have ID card* - Never *has*) |
